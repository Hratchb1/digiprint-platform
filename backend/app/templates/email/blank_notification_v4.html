Why would my film turn out blank?

Extreme underexposure

Very fast shutter, a very small aperture, or ISO set far too high can leave frames effectively blank.

Tip Match ISO to the film box speed and use a light meter or auto mode in low light. If this was a disposable camera, photos were most likely taken in low light without flash.

Unshot roll was developed

A fresh roll got processed by mistake. The result is completely clear film with edge markings only.

Tip Mark exposed rolls clearly and keep fresh rolls in their canisters until ready to shoot.

Camera opened mid-roll

Opening the camera back floods the film with light. You might see uniform fog rather than images.

Tip Only open the back after fully rewinding the film.

Shutter didn't fire or is stuck

A mechanical fault or a low battery on electronic shutters can prevent any exposure.

Tip Replace the battery and fire a few test shots before loading your next roll.

Film didn't load or advance

The leader didn't catch on the take-up spool. The counter moved, but the film never actually advanced.

Tip After loading, gently tug the rewind knob — resistance means the film is advancing correctly.
