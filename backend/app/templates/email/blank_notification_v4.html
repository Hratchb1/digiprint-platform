Why can this happen?

1. The film was extremely underexposed
Not enough light reached the film — often from a fast shutter speed, the wrong ISO setting, or shooting in low light without a flash on a disposable camera.
2. The film wasn't loaded correctly
The leader didn't engage with the take-up spool. The camera appeared to advance normally, but the film never actually moved through it.
3. An unshot roll was processed
A fresh, unused roll was accidentally sent in for developing. The negatives come back completely clear aside from edge markings.
4. The camera experienced a mechanical issue
A stuck shutter, faulty film advance, or a flat battery on an electronic camera can all stop frames from exposing properly.
5. The camera was opened before the film was rewound
Opening the back before rewinding exposes the whole roll to light.

If you're unsure what happened, simply reply to this email and let us know the camera model, the film used, whether photos were taken indoors or outdoors, and anything else you remember. We'll gladly help work out the most likely cause.
